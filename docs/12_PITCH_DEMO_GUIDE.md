# 12 — Pitch demonstration guide

Version 2.0 | Use after G3 passes. This is a private demonstration with synthetic data and simulated payments.

## Objective

Show how the owner can publish products, accept remote orders and keep the website aligned with shop sales. Demonstrate operational value instead of presenting a long feature list or promising revenue growth.

## Preparation

Use 12–20 clearly labelled sample products with lotion, perfume, makeup and accessories. Include at least two size/shade variants, one low-stock SKU and an expiry/quarantine example. Use approved photos or neutral placeholders. Reset the demo only in its designated development database; never reset real stock.

Prepare an owner demo account using actual authentication, a phone-sized browser view and the staff dashboard. Avoid exposing credentials, real customer details or privileged settings. The screen must make simulated payment status obvious.

## Demonstration sequence

| Step | Show | Business point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Browse products on a phone | Customers can inspect the catalogue remotely |
| 2 | Select size/shade and see its price | Orders identify the actual item |
| 3 | Checkout with pickup or a demo delivery zone | The customer sees a final total |
| 4 | Complete a labelled simulated payment | The workflow can be demonstrated without collecting money |
| 5 | Open the order in staff view | Staff have one clear queue and order record |
| 6 | Prepare and hand over the item | The order and stock history remain connected |
| 7 | Record a separate shop sale | Online availability reflects physical-store activity |
| 8 | Show low-stock/expiry information | Staff can act before a stock problem surprises a customer |

## Discussion and next agreement

Ask whether the workflow fits their daily work, who records sales, which reports they actually use and what delivery promises they can fulfil. Document necessary changes without adding every suggested feature to the first release.

Explain that live provider integration and production readiness follow the demonstration. Agree the paid pilot scope, deposit/payment terms, product upload responsibility, support, running costs and business ownership. Do not imply the mock payment proves bank settlement.

Use pilot measures from the PRD: fulfilled orders, stock-related cancellations, payment exceptions, handoff time and staff recording consistency. Request permission separately before using the business's identity, screenshots or results in public marketing.
